The current `/home-2` is strong as a **brand-and-entry page**, but it is too dense and contains several elements that now belong on dedicated pages: Consulting, Case Studies, Company, and Industries. The best final homepage should be shorter, more decisive, and route visitors into the new sitemap quickly. 

## Overall evaluation

The page already expresses an excellent OXOT proposition:

> **See your OT environment. Understand the risk. Know what to do next.**

That is a strong homepage line because it is simpler than the Platform-page promise. The Platform page can then explain *how* the Cyber Digital Twin makes those decisions possible.

The current homepage succeeds at:

- Establishing Dutch OT-security positioning immediately.
- Framing OXOT around operational consequence rather than generic security.
- Introducing the Cyber Digital Twin and consulting together.
- Showing decision-led differentiation.
- Demonstrating that OXOT has real technical work and real industry relevance.
- Providing a working hero conversion route to the global Contact Band and the Twin page. 

Its weakness is **scope creep**. The home page is trying to become a Platform page, Consulting page, Case Studies index, Collaboration page, and Industry hub in one long scroll.

## What is working well

### 1. Hero positioning

The hero says:

```text
See your OT environment.
Understand the risk.
Know what to do next.
```

This is ideal **homepage language**. It is not overtechnical, but it is still credible to a CISO, plant manager, architect, board member, or engineering lead.

The supporting line:

> “For the industries that cannot afford to stop.”

is also right. It reinforces OXOT’s market without listing sectors too early. 

I would preserve this exact structure:

```text
Dutch OT cybersecurity
See your OT environment.
Understand the risk.
Know what to do next.

For the industries that cannot afford to stop.
```

The body copy is strong but slightly too dense. Simplify it:

> OXOT builds a Cyber Digital Twin from the engineering evidence you already hold. Model cyber pathways, test changes safely, and make decisions your engineers can act on and your board can defend.

That is easier to absorb than “attack it, change it and price it” as the first explanation. Keep “attack it, change it and price it” lower on the page or in a more technical product context.

### 2. The three-part trust strip

The current hero strip is excellent:

```text
SEE
The plant as it actually is.

UNDERSTAND
Consequence before severity.

DECIDE
A queue, and permission to ignore.
```

This is concise, differentiated, and memorable. It should remain directly below the hero. 
I would adjust the last item from “permission to ignore” to:

> **A queue—and a defensible decision to defer.**

This preserves the powerful point without sounding careless to regulated, safety, rail, defense, and water audiences.

### 3. The four decisions

The four decision cards are the strongest conversion bridge from the homepage to the Platform/Decisions area:

```text
What do we fix first?
What should we spend?
Can we change this safely?
What can we ignore?
```

They correctly communicate that OXOT sells **decision confidence**, not generic assessment output. 

However, the section has no H2. Add one:

> ## Four decisions that make OT security actionable.

Then introduce the four cards:

> The Cyber Digital Twin turns disconnected engineering, security, and operational evidence into four decisions your organization has to make anyway.

This fixes both accessibility hierarchy and narrative clarity.

### 4. “A model, and the engineers who use it”

This is a very good section concept:

> **A model, and the engineers who use it.**

It solves a core commercial ambiguity:

```text
Is OXOT a software vendor?
Is OXOT a consultancy?
Is OXOT both?
```

The answer should be:

> **The Cyber Digital Twin is the product. OXOT consulting makes it reliable in your environment.**

Keep this section, but use the new page destinations:

| Card | New destination |
|---|---|
| Cyber Digital Twin | `/platform` |
| Consulting Services | `/consulting` |

The existing copy is good, but the current link text about “what the Dutch government funded” is too grant-centric for the central product route. Use:

```text
Explore the Cyber Digital Twin
Explore Consulting Services
```

The CIF-NL support can remain as a small credibility fact elsewhere, not the central product CTA.

### 5. The Cyber Digital Twin section

The headline:

> **You cannot defend what you have never modelled.**

is excellent. It should stay.

The four technical disciplines are also strong:

```text
Engineering-accurate facility model
What-if scenario simulation
Safety-reliability-cyber convergence
Capital investment prioritisation
```

This is enough technical proof for the homepage. 

The “test the control” subsection also does the job well, especially because it shows a before/after logic rather than a static digital-twin claim.

But this section should be shorter. Its job is to encourage a visit to the Platform page, not explain the entire engine.

Use this homepage version:

```text
You cannot defend what you have never modelled.

A Cyber Digital Twin connects the process, the asset, the network pathway,
the supplier dependency, and the consequence of failure in one working model.

- Build from engineering evidence.
- Trace real cyber reachability.
- Test a firewall, patch, supplier, or access change before production.
- Keep the evidence behind the decision.

[Explore the Cyber Digital Twin]
```

Then retain one visual:

```text
Baseline → virtual control → pathways closed → consequence reduced
```

### 6. Case Studies section

The current heading:

> **Work we have actually done.**

is exactly the right tone. It is much stronger than “Success Stories” or “Customer Stories.” 

However, 10 cards on the homepage are too many. The homepage should show **three featured case studies** and one link:

```text
Featured Work
- OT M&A due diligence under deal-clock pressure
- Signaling vendor-access and railway recovery
- Plant remote-access and segmentation design

[Explore all Case Studies]
```

Use only case studies that are approved, named, anonymized appropriately, and genuinely published. Do not show a large catalogue just because the routes exist.

### 7. Global Contact Band

The global Contact Band is a valid final CTA, and the page correctly relies on it. 

The hero primary CTA already scrolls to it, which works technically. But for a complex B2B buyer journey, I would make the hero CTA go directly to `/contact` instead.

Use:

```text
Primary:
Talk to an OT Engineer

Secondary:
Explore the Cyber Digital Twin
```

A page anchor is acceptable, but a dedicated contact route is easier to share, measure, bookmark, and use after a visitor has navigated elsewhere.

## What should change

### 1. Remove the Partners / Collaboration section

The current section is titled:

> “Nobody secures a plant alone.”

The philosophy is true, but the website now intentionally has no Partners page and does not want to name partners or employees. The existing call to action also points to a non-existent `/collaboration` page. 
**Recommendation: remove this section from the homepage entirely.**

Reuse its best underlying message on the Consulting page:

> OXOT works with your engineers, operators, safety and reliability teams, IT, procurement, suppliers, and service providers. A security decision that ignores any of them does not survive contact with a live environment.

That message belongs under **How We Work**, not as a homepage collaboration section.

### 2. Move detailed consulting off the homepage

The full six-service expandable grid is excellent content, but it is too deep for the homepage now that Consulting has its own page. 

Replace it with a compact section:

```text
Consulting built around the Twin

Decision Sprint
Answer one urgent risk, change, investment, or supplier question.

Twin Build
Create a durable model for a facility, system, product, or estate.

Continuous Twin Operations
Keep pace with operational, supplier, technology, and threat change.

[Explore Consulting Services]
```

The full six cards should live on `/consulting`.

### 3. Update industry section to six real destinations

The current homepage lists only:

```text
Manufacturing
Water
Energy
Transportation
```

That no longer matches the site strategy. It must become:

```text
Manufacturing & Process
Energy & Utilities
Water & Wastewater
Rail & Transportation
Hyperscale & Data Centers
Defense & Government
```

Each needs a proper linked page. 
The section headline can stay:

> **The industries that cannot afford to stop.**

But add supporting copy:

> The system differs. The decision does not: understand what matters, test what changes, and retain the evidence behind the action.

### 4. Move company / grant detail out

The existing Company section has valuable messages:

- OT engineers, not IT security.
- A number, not an adjective.
- What is true, not what is good for us.
- Your data and your model stay yours.
- Engagements designed to end. 

These are excellent company principles, but the full section is too detailed for the homepage.

Use a condensed trust strip under the hero or after the four decisions:

```text
Engineering-led
Former Fox-IT and NCC Group OT security leads.

Vendor-neutral
Evidence chooses the control—not a reseller agreement.

Sovereign by design
Your data and model remain under your control.

Passive-first
No agents on controllers. No active production-network scanning.
```

Then link to `/company`.

The CIF-NL grant receipt panel should move off the homepage or become a discreet note. It is useful credibility, but it should not be visually equal to the product story.

### 5. Remove deep links to comparison routes

The current homepage points internally to `/cdt-2#risk` and `/cdt-2#test`, while its larger CTAs point to `/twin`. This creates unclear page ownership. 
Once the final Platform page is chosen:

```text
All product links should route to /platform
All consulting links should route to /consulting
All assurance links should route to /assurance
All industry links should route to /industries/[vertical]
```

Do not expose `/cdt-2`, `/home-2`, or comparison-build routes in production navigation or content.

### 6. Make the homepage shorter

Current page flow:

```text
Hero
Four Decisions
Company
Two Ways In
Twin
Six Consulting Services
Case Studies
Partners
Sectors
Global Contact Band
```

This is good material, but it repeats similar concepts:

- Company principles and Two Ways In overlap.
- Twin and Four Decisions overlap.
- Consulting appears in Two Ways In and then again in full.
- Partners overlaps with consulting method.
- Case studies are too numerous.
- Sector list is incomplete.

## Homepage versus Platform

The distinction needs to stay sharp:

| Homepage | Platform |
|---|---|
| “What is OXOT, and why should I care?” | “What is the Cyber Digital Twin, and how does it work?” |
| Simple promise and trust | Full product narrative and technical proof |
| Four decisions at a high level | Detailed NOW/NEXT/accepted, investment, simulation, and evidence logic |
| One visual/system scenario | Worked examples, seven layers, lenses, data inputs, BOMs, deployment |
| Direct routes to Platform, Consulting, Industries, Case Studies | Direct route to technical briefing, specification, deployment, assurance |
| Short, high-confidence entry page | Longer technical-evaluation page |

The home page should make a visitor want to explore. The Platform page should make a technical buyer confident enough to engage.
