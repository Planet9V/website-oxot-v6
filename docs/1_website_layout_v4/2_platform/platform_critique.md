The current `/cdt-2` Platform page has a **very strong core narrative** and is closely aligned with the product-first direction we defined. Its biggest issue is not what it says; it is that it is trying to carry too much of the website alone—including consulting, deployment, assurance, company provenance, and industry routing—while missing a few conversion and structural refinements. 

## Overall assessment

**Keep the strategic spine.** The page correctly leads with outcomes and buyer decisions before exposing the technical engine:

```text
Problem
→ What decisions the Twin answers
→ Why the answers are credible
→ Worked example
→ External context
→ Engine and model
→ Consulting and deployment
→ FAQ
```

That is better than leading with “seven layers,” AI, graphs, protocols, or generic digital-twin features. It directly supports OXOT’s strongest message:

> **Before you change and spend, replicate your plant.**

The Hero, the four decisions, the “consequence then pathway then probability then price” logic, and the worked dosing-controller example all fit the desired Platform-page positioning exceptionally well.

## What is working especially well

### 1. The opening proposition

The hero is right:

> “Before you change and spend, replicate your plant.”

It is short, distinctive, operational, and commercially useful. The supporting statement that customers are buying the ability to decide “what to fix, what to spend and what to leave alone” is also very strong. It avoids selling “a model” for its own sake. 

Keep this exact concept as the Platform H1 and central positioning.

### 2. The decision-first story

The current sequence makes the four OXOT decisions feel like the product:

1. What do we fix first?
2. What should we spend?
3. Can we change this safely?
4. What can we ignore?

This is exactly the right commercial framing. It separates OXOT from OT monitoring, asset inventory, scanner, vulnerability-management, SOC, and dashboard vendors. 

The **NOW / NEXT / NEVER** model is particularly distinctive. Keep it, but use language carefully:

```text
NOW
A reachable route can affect a critical function.

NEXT
A real exposure should be addressed in planned work.

ACCEPTED / DEFERRED
A documented decision with rationale, compensating controls,
an accountable owner, and a review trigger.
```

I recommend changing the website-facing label from **“NEVER”** to:

> **Accepted / deferred under controlled conditions**

Internally, the “NEVER” logic can remain useful. Publicly, especially for regulated, rail, water, energy, government, and safety-minded buyers, “never” can sound overly absolute.

### 3. The consequence-led risk explanation

The current section:

> “Consequence, then probability, then price.”

is one of the best parts of the page. The four-step chain is clear:

```text
Consequence
→ Pathway
→ Probability
→ Financial impact
```

It expresses the difference between OXOT and generic vulnerability scoring. It also supports your IEC 62443, TS 50701, IEC 62278-2:2025, CRA, and evidence/provenance positioning. 

### 4. The worked example

The dosing-controller example is essential. It proves the idea without requiring the buyer to understand the entire architecture:

```text
Vulnerability
→ Reachable vendor route
→ Controller / process effect
→ Operational consequence
→ Cheapest effective control
```

This should remain on the Platform page. It is the bridge between abstract claims and a credible OXOT decision. It also gives the visitor a mental model for how the Twin behaves. 
### 5. The “engine comes later” structure

The page does not lead with the seven-layer technical architecture. That is correct.

The current order:

```text
Decisions first
→ Why the answers hold
→ Engine
→ Seven layers
→ Lenses
```

is stronger than the traditional product-page order:

```text
Architecture
→ Features
→ Protocols
→ Integrations
→ Maybe outcomes
```

Keep that logic.

### 6. Passive-first deployment

The deployment section is well positioned late in the technical evaluation phase. It addresses a critical objection after the visitor understands why the product matters:

```text
Island Mode
One-way data diode
Dedicated instance
```

The passive-first statement—no agents on controllers and no active process-network scanning—should remain highly visible. ## Bottom line

The current Platform page is **substantively excellent**. It already has the correct OXOT story:

> Engineering evidence → consequence → pathway → decision → virtual test → traceable action.

The final changes are about **discipline and routing**, not a rewrite:

- Fix CTAs.
- Add final conversion.
- Keep decisions, worked example, risk logic, technical proof, and deployment.
- Move detailed consulting to Consulting.
- Move detailed assurance to Assurance.
- Move company origin to About.
- Link strongly to Industries, Resources, and Contact.
- Consolidate the engine sequence so technical sophistication does not become page fatigue.

The result remains product-first, but makes clear that OXOT consulting is how the product becomes reliable and useful in a real customer environment.
