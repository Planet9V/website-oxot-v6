## 1. Claroty — OT/CPS network visibility & protection (adjacent, complementary)

**Positioning (2026):** Rebranding from "OT security" to "cyber-physical systems (CPS) protection," spanning industrial, healthcare, and commercial building systems. Tagline in current messaging: *"Accelerating your CPS cybersecurity journey."* [Claroty — 2026 Predictions webinar](https://claroty.com/resources/webinars/2026-predictions-for-ot-security-and-the-future-of-cyber-physical-systems); [Claroty 10-year milestone release](https://claroty.com/press-releases/claroty-celebrates-ten-years-of-industry-leading-protection-for-the-worlds-mission-critical-cyber-physical-systems)

**What they do:** Network-level asset discovery, passive/active monitoring, and threat detection across OT/ICS, IoT, and now broader CPS environments (healthcare devices, building automation).

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Established 10-year track record, named enterprise customers, broad protocol/device coverage, deep channel/partner network (e.g., missile-defense/intelligence facility deployments cited in recent press). Real-time network visibility and detection — a live-monitoring capability OXOT does not claim to provide.

**Where OXOT differs:** Claroty tells you what's on the network and what's talking to what. It does not build a P&ID-level physics twin of the facility, and it does not compute a dollar-denominated ALE figure. OXOT's internal positioning treats Claroty's output as an input OXOT ingests (named explicitly in the source corpus: "Claroty, Nozomi Networks, Dragos, Armis" listed as ecosystem integrations, not rivals).

**Opportunity for OXOT:** "We don't replace your Claroty deployment — we turn what it already sees into a board-legible number and a prioritized fix list."

## 2. Dragos — OT-native threat intelligence & detection (adjacent, complementary)

**Positioning (2026):** OT-native, built by defenders with real incident-response field experience; emphasizes threat intelligence pedigree and "complete xOT cybersecurity without disrupting operations" across 600+ ICS protocols. [Dragos platform brochure, Feb 2026](https://dragos.brightspotcdn.com/c2/d7/958e53fc4950877e6332124619aa/dragos-br-platform-feb26-r2-2026.pdf); [Dragos platform page](https://www.dragos.com/cybersecurity-platform/)

**What they do:** OT threat detection, incident response, and a widely-cited annual threat intelligence report (used as a source of named-actor/industry-trend data across the OT security industry, including — per this session's earlier extraction — cited indirectly via shared reference points like Volt Typhoon activity).

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Named threat intelligence brand recognition (their annual "Year in Review" report is an industry reference point), incident-response services, deep protocol coverage claim (600+).

**Where OXOT differs:** Dragos profiles threats generically by sector; OXOT's TACAM/ATQ system (per the methodology corpus) attributes threats down to the specific vendor product installed at a specific facility — a narrower, more procurement-relevant claim Dragos does not make in its public positioning.

**Opportunity for OXOT:** Position TACAM's product-level attribution as the layer above Dragos's sector-level threat intel — "Dragos tells you an actor targets your sector; TACAM tells you if they can exploit the specific PLC you already own."

## 3. Nozomi Networks — OT/IoT visibility, Gartner CPS Leader (adjacent, complementary)

**Positioning (2026):** "The Leader in OT Cybersecurity Technology," emphasizing deployment flexibility (broadest range of form factors) and AI-driven analytics across distributed, multi-site industrial environments. Named a Leader in Gartner's first Magic Quadrant for CPS Protection Platforms (Feb 2025). [Nozomi Networks platform page](https://www.nozominetworks.com/platform); [Nozomi Networks homepage](https://www.nozominetworks.com/)

**What they do:** OT/IoT asset discovery, inventory, continuous monitoring, and AI-powered anomaly analysis, with a stated goal of "minimize cyber risk, maximize operational resilience, streamline compliance."

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Analyst-recognized market leadership (Gartner MQ Leader), largest claimed device-coverage breadth, proven at scale across large geographically-distributed operators.

**Where OXOT differs:** Nozomi's "streamline compliance" claim is closest to OXOT's own compliance-as-a-byproduct positioning — worth watching as a potential message overlap. OXOT's differentiator remains the financial-quantification layer (ALE) and the physics/P&ID-level asset model, neither of which Nozomi's public positioning claims.

**Opportunity for OXOT:** Nozomi is the most credible incumbent to name-check as "the network visibility layer we sit above," given its analyst-recognized leadership — borrowing their credibility while being explicit OXOT isn't competing for the same budget line.

## 4. Armis — Cyber exposure management, now under ServiceNow (adjacent, complementary, higher risk of overlap)

**Positioning (2026):** "Cyber Exposure Management," an agentless asset-intelligence platform correlating data across corporate IT, OT, IoT, medical, and building systems into one unified exposure view. Named a 2026 Gartner MQ Leader for CPS Protection Platforms (2nd consecutive year); recently acquired by ServiceNow. [Armis homepage](https://www.armis.com/); [Armis 2026 Gartner MQ Leader announcement](https://www.armis.com/blog/armis-named-a-leader-in-the-2026-gartner-magic-quadrant-for-cps-protection-platforms-for-the-2nd-consecutive-year/); [ServiceNow/Armis acquisition](https://industrialcyber.co/news/servicenow-closes-armis-deal-to-extend-ai-powered-cyber-risk-visibility-across-ot-and-iot/)

**What they do:** Broad, agentless asset visibility and risk correlation across converged IT/OT/IoT/medical/building environments — explicitly positioned as an enterprise-wide exposure platform, not OT-only.

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Now backed by ServiceNow's enterprise distribution and ITSM install base — the single biggest go-to-market scale advantage of anything in this list. "Exposure management" framing already touches on prioritization language ("what to fix first") that overlaps conceptually with OXOT's NOW/NEXT/NEVER triage.

**Where OXOT differs:** Armis is converged-IT-first with OT as one of several asset classes; OXOT is OT/physics-first with a P&ID-level facility model Armis's network/asset-correlation approach doesn't build. Armis does not claim an actuarial-grade ALE computation.

**Opportunity / watch item:** Of the four adjacent vendors, Armis's "exposure management" positioning is the closest conceptual neighbor to OXOT's prioritization story — worth monitoring for message overlap as Armis's ServiceNow-backed distribution scales, even though the underlying architecture (converged asset correlation vs. physics-based facility twin) is genuinely different.

## 5. Axio (Axio360) — Cyber risk quantification, direct-adjacent

**Positioning:** "Dollar-based clarity on every cyber risk... decide with evidence, not instinct." Supports FAIR and its own proprietary quantification framework; subscription pricing roughly **$50K–$150K/year** by org size. Target industries: critical infrastructure, energy, financial services, healthcare, manufacturing. [Axio homepage](https://axio.com/)

**What they do:** Cyber risk assessments, financial risk quantification, risk-transfer (insurance) optimization, security-program performance planning — output is dollar-denominated risk, the same category OXOT's ALE metric sits in.

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Established enterprise pricing/packaging, multi-methodology flexibility (not locked to one quantification model), proven in financial-services and general-enterprise risk teams, not just OT.

**Where OXOT differs — this is the closest true secondary competitor in the set:** Axio's quantification is a standalone risk-management layer applied by an analyst to whatever inputs a customer provides. OXOT's ALE is generated automatically from a physics-based facility twin built from real P&ID/engineering drawings — the quantification is a byproduct of the twin, not a separate analyst exercise. Axio is not OT-native; its published target industries are broader (financial services, healthcare) and its methodology isn't grounded in plant-physics/safety-engineering data (FMECA, SIL, hazard logs) the way OXOT's is.

**Opportunity for OXOT:** "Axio quantifies the risk you tell it about. OXOT builds the facility model that tells you what the risk actually is, then quantifies it — no analyst interview required."

## 6. RiskLens (now part of Safe Security) — FAIR-standard cyber risk quantification, direct-adjacent

**Positioning:** "The only enterprise software platform purpose-built on FAIR," serving Fortune 1000 and government. Acquired by Safe Security in 2025, forming what's marketed as "the most comprehensive FAIR-based CRQ platform on the market" — a genuine consolidation signal in this category. [RiskLens FAIR page](https://www.risklens.com/cyber-risk-quantification/the-fair-standard); [Safe Security/RiskLens acquisition](https://www.paladincapgroup.com/safe-security-acquires-risklens-to-become-undisputed-leader-in-the-4b-cyber-risk-quantification-and-management-crqm-market/)

**What they do:** FAIR-model risk quantification, Monte Carlo scenario simulation, prioritization by economic impact — methodologically the closest public analog to OXOT's own ALE/Monte Carlo approach, minus the OT-physics grounding.

**Where they're strong vs. OXOT:** Standards-body legitimacy (FAIR is a Open Group standard, a credibility asset OXOT's internally-built methodology doesn't have an equivalent for), enterprise-scale customer base, now backed by Safe Security's broader platform and go-to-market.

**Where OXOT differs:** Same structural gap as Axio — RiskLens quantifies risk from whatever data a customer's team inputs; it is not grounded in a physics-based OT facility twin, real P&ID ingestion, or plant safety-engineering data. RiskLens is IT/enterprise-risk-native, not OT-native.

**Opportunity for OXOT:** The FAIR-vs-OXOT-methodology question ("why not just use the industry-standard FAIR model?") is a real, anticipatable objection this profile surfaces that the objections table in the product-marketing context doesn't yet cover — recommend adding it before Track 1 copywriting begins.

## Key Takeaways

1. **OXOT has no true head-to-head competitor identified in this scan.** The four OT-monitoring vendors (Claroty, Dragos, Nozomi, Armis) don't compute financial risk from a physics-based facility twin; the two quantification vendors (Axio, RiskLens) don't build an OT-native, P&ID-grounded facility model. OXOT's own "complementary layer" framing is defensible and should stay the default posture in copy — this is a category-creation position, not a displacement pitch.
2. **Armis is the vendor most likely to converge into overlapping territory over time.** Its "exposure management"/prioritization language and newly-acquired ServiceNow distribution scale make it worth monitoring, even though its architecture (converged-asset correlation) is still structurally different from OXOT's physics-twin approach.
3. **Axio and RiskLens are the closest real secondary competitors** — both do dollar-denominated cyber risk quantification, the same category as OXOT's ALE. The differentiator to lean on is that OXOT's number comes from an automatically-built facility twin, not an analyst-run FAIR workshop. RiskLens's FAIR-standard legitimacy is a real objection ("why not just use FAIR?") not yet covered in the product-marketing objections table — recommend adding before copywriting.
4. **RiskLens/Safe Security's 2025 acquisition signals active consolidation in the cyber-risk-quantification category** — worth noting as market context (the category is being taken seriously enough to consolidate), not a direct threat to OXOT's OT-specific niche.
5. **"Doing nothing" (spreadsheets, annual pen tests, no formal quantification) is very likely OXOT's largest real-world competitor by volume**, and is the one competitor this entire research pass — internal source material included — has the least developed messaging against. Recommend this as a specific gap for Track 1 copywriting: a "why now, why not just keep doing what we're doing" argument.
