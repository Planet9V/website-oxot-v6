THE METHOD
BUILD · ENRICH · SIMULATE · PRIORITISE

We build the model. Then we break it — ten thousand times.

An engineering twin tells you what exists. A cyber digital twin tells you what it costs you when it fails, how likely that is this year, and which three things to fix first.

01BUILD the as-is
Geometry, topology and control layer, reconciled against drawings, configuration and a site walk. The twin becomes the agreed statement of what is actually installed.
02ENRICH with the BOM
Every object inherits its hardware, software, cryptographic and service bill of materials, cross-referenced continuously against live vulnerability and threat intelligence.
03SIMULATE the consequence
Threat-model each pathway, breach the minimum operating requirements, and run the outcome distribution — not a single worst case, and not a heat-map colour.
04PRIORITISE the spend
Rank every finding by loss avoided per euro spent, and defend that ranking in front of a board, an insurer or a regulator.

STEP 04 · THE OUTPUT
A DEFENSIBLE SPENDING ORDER

NOW · NEXT · NEVER

Not a severity score. A ranking derived from reachability in the twin, exploitability in the wild, and loss avoided per euro spent.

NOW
Reachable, exploited, breaks a minimum requirement.
Known-exploited vulnerability on an asset with a live pathway to a safety or cooling function. Highest loss avoided per euro. Fix inside the change window.
NEXT
Real, but bounded — or the path is long.
Meaningful ALE reduction that does not justify an emergency outage. Scheduled into the next maintenance window, with a compensating control until then.
NEVER
Unreachable, or the fix costs more than the loss.
Accepted with reasoning recorded against the object in the twin. The most valuable column — it is what lets your team defensibly stop working on the other 400 findings.
Re-run it when anything changes.
New CVE, new firmware, new tenant load, a decommissioned UPS — the pathway analysis and the ALE move with the plant, because they were never separate from it.
