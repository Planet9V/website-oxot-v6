# Headline
> Security tools that don't know what a valve does

## Subheadline
OT and industrial security inherited its playbook from IT — firewalls, patch cadence, network
monitoring. None of it was built to understand that patching this system might disable a safety
interlock, or that this "low-severity" vulnerability sits on the one asset that would shut down your
whole facility if it failed.

---

## The gap

A vulnerability scanner can tell you a device is running old firmware. It can't tell you that device
also happens to control cooling for your most critical row of equipment — or that a failure there
cascades into three other systems before anyone notices.

That gap — between what a security tool sees and what actually happens on your plant floor — is where
real risk hides. Not because anyone missed something. Because the tool was never built to see it in
the first place.

## What this costs teams, concretely

Without a model of physical consequence, every security decision becomes a negotiation nobody can win
cleanly: patch now and risk the maintenance window, or wait and carry the exposure. Isolate the
system, or keep the safety logic intact.

These decisions get made — but usually on judgment and urgency, not on an actual number for what's
really at stake.

## What closes the gap

A model that understands both sides — the cyber exposure *and* the physical engineering behind it —
turns that negotiation into a calculation. Not "patch or don't." *Here's what patching costs you.
Here's what waiting costs you. Here's the actual number for each.*
