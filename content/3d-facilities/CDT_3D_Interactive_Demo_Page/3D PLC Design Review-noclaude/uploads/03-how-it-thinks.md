# Headline
> A real number, not a checklist score

## Subheadline
Most security and compliance tools give you a score against a generic standard — a percentage, a
letter grade, a checklist completion rate. OXOT gives you something a budget conversation can
actually use: a defensible number, built from your real equipment and its real failure history — not
an industry average.

---

## How it's built

Underneath, the twin runs the same kind of engineering analysis reliability and safety engineers
already trust — failure mode analysis, reliability figures, hazard assessment — and combines it with
live threat intelligence: which vulnerabilities are actively exploited, which ones matter for your
specific equipment, and which ones are just noise.

The result isn't a guess dressed up as a score. It's a calculation: how likely is this, and what would
it actually cost.

*(If you're the kind of engineer who wants the specifics: this is FMECA and reliability analysis,
IEC 62443 zone and target security-level modeling, and CVE/exploit-intelligence matching, feeding an
actuarial-style loss calculation. Happy to walk through the full methodology in a briefing.)*

## Why "generic best practice" isn't enough

An industry checklist tells you what most facilities like yours should probably do. It can't tell you
what *your* facility should do — because it doesn't know your equipment, your layout, your history.
Two facilities can look identical on a compliance checklist and have completely different actual
risk. The twin is built from your plant, not a template of one.

## What you get instead of an alert queue

Instead of a queue of alerts ranked by generic severity, you get a ranked list of what to fix, priced
in the terms that matter to whoever's asking — downtime hours for operations, dollars for finance,
safety margin for engineering.
