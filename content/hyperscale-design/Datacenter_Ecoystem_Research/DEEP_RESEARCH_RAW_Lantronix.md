## 5. REGULATORY EXPOSURE AND COMPLIANCE

### 5.1 EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Scope

The Cyber Resilience Act, adopted December 2024 and effective September 11, 2026, establishes mandatory cybersecurity requirements for all "products with digital elements" sold in the EU market [[47]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act).

**Lantronix Applicability:** Products including network-connected embedded modules (xPico series), IoT gateways (SGX 5150), routers (NTC-500, NTC-550), console managers (SLC 9000, SLC 8000), and software services (Percepxion, Kompress.ai) all qualify as "products with digital elements" under Article 3(1) of the CRA [[47]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act).

**Classification (Class I vs. Class II):** The CRA establishes a two-class system with Class II products subject to stricter conformity assessment requirements. Lantronix has NOT publicly disclosed which products qualify as Class I (low-risk) versus Class II (higher-risk). This determination affects compliance timelines and certification pathways [[47]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act).

**Software Bill of Materials Requirement:** CRA mandates SBOMs for all products by December 11, 2027 [[47]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act). As noted above, Lantronix currently does not publish SBOMs [[41]](https://www.lantronix.com/technical-support/security-updates/).

**Vulnerability Disclosure Timeline:** The CRA effective September 11, 2026 creates a 24-hour "proof-of-concept period" for vulnerability reporters; Lantronix must align its incident response processes to this compressed timeline [[42]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-203-02) [[16]](https://www.lantronix.com/technical-support/security-updates/vulnerability-disclosure-policy/).

### 5.2 NIS2 Directive (EU Network and Information Systems Security)

**Scope:** The NIS2 Directive, effective October 17, 2024, applies to "essential and important entities" defined as organizations with more than 50 employees or revenue exceeding €10 million [[44]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[46]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive).

**Lantronix Classification:** Lantronix qualifies as an "important entity" under NIS2, as it employs 469 personnel and generated \$122.9 million in FY2026 revenue, far exceeding both thresholds [[44]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[46]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive).

**Compliance Requirements:** As an important entity, Lantronix must implement risk management measures, conduct security impact assessments, report incidents within 72 hours, and maintain supply chain security [[44]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[45]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/).

**Compliance Deadline:** October 2026 — now in force [[44]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[46]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive).

### 5.3 NERC CIP (Energy Sector Cyber Security)

**Applicability:** NERC CIP standards govern bulk power system operators and their critical digital assets [[49]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0935/ML093510905.pdf) [[50]](https://blogs.dnvgl.com/energy/cyber-security-nerc-compliance-and-the-nuclear-plant-challenge). Lantronix does not operate or control electric utility infrastructure and therefore **is not directly subject to NERC CIP**. However, if Lantronix products are deployed within NERC-regulated entities' critical infrastructure, those customers assume NERC compliance responsibility [[49]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0935/ML093510905.pdf) [[50]](https://blogs.dnvgl.com/energy/cyber-security-nerc-compliance-and-the-nuclear-plant-challenge).

### 5.4 NRC 10 CFR 73.54 (Nuclear Sector Cyber Security)

**Applicability:** The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission regulation 10 CFR § 73.54 governs digital systems in commercial nuclear power reactor operations [[49]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0935/ML093510905.pdf) [[51]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ml1011/ml101180437.pdf).

**Lantronix Status:** Lantronix does not currently market products explicitly for nuclear power plant applications in available disclosures. However, Lantronix does serve the "energy sector" broadly [[10]](https://www.globaldata.com/company-profile/lantronix-inc/), and critical asset monitoring hardware (FOX Series, BOLERO40 Series) could theoretically be deployed at nuclear facilities. No explicit NRC certification or compliance pathway is documented [[49]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0935/ML093510905.pdf) [[51]](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ml1011/ml101180437.pdf).

### 10.2 Critical Infrastructure Risk Assessment

**Highest Risk: CVE-2025-2567 (XPort Authentication Bypass)**

The XPort authentication bypass vulnerability (CVSS 9.8) represents the most severe current risk to Lantronix customers because:

1. **Sector Exposure:** XPort devices are deployed in fuel storage and tank monitoring systems (Automated Tank Gauge, or ATG) globally [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)
2. **Attacker Capability:** Remote, unauthenticated access to critical infrastructure without patches [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)
3. **Operational Impact:** Attackers can disable tank monitoring, creating fuel supply chain disruption and safety hazards [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)
4. **Affected Sectors:** Energy, Critical Manufacturing, Transportation, Water/Wastewater (all U.S. critical infrastructure designations) [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)
5. **Known Exploited:** The vulnerability has been weaponized (CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog inclusion likely) [[43]](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog?field_cve=CVE-2025-34026)

**Mitigation Path:**
- Immediate: Patch to XPort firmware v8.0.0.0 [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)
- Long-term: Migrate to XPort Edge (architecturally secure) [[13]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-105-05)

### 10.3 Vulnerability Disclosure Process and Timing

Lantronix's Vulnerability Disclosure Policy (v2.0, effective March 27, 2025) establishes [[16]](https://www.lantronix.com/technical-support/security-updates/vulnerability-disclosure-policy/):

- **Initial Response:** 5 business days for vulnerability investigation
- **Patch Determination:** 10 business days for security fix decision
- **Target Resolution:** 90-day window for patch release
- **Scoring Standard:** NIST CVSS scoring methodology
- **Publication Channel:** lantronix.com/technical-support/security-updates [[41]](https://www.lantronix.com/technical-support/security-updates/)

This process aligns with industry standards but is slower than the new CRA requirement of 24-hour "proof-of-concept" notification (effective September 11, 2026) [[42]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-203-02).

### 10.4 Product Lifecycle and Security Support

**End-of-Support Products with Unpatched CVEs:**

1. **Device Installer v4.4.0.7** — EOS 2018; CVE-2025-4338 XXE vulnerability; no patches available; users must migrate to Provisioning Manager [[14]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-142-01)
2. **XPort (Original)** — Legacy product; CVE-2023-7237 weak encoding; no patches; users must migrate to XPort Edge [[15]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-24-023-05)

**Assessment:** Organizations still using Device Installer or legacy XPort must urgently upgrade to eliminate critical vulnerabilities with no vendor fix available.

### 10.5 PSIRT and Incident Response Coordination

## CONCLUSION

Lantronix represents a mid-market provider undergoing strategic transformation from traditional out-of-band management toward edge AI and defense applications. The organization demonstrates technical depth in IoT and network management but carries material cybersecurity compliance gaps (missing ISO 27001, SOC 2, IEC 62443 certifications) and organizational security function deficiencies (no published CISO, no PSIRT). Recent critical vulnerabilities in deployed critical infrastructure products (XPort CVSS 9.8) require customers to maintain active patch management and consider product migration strategies. The company's financial profile shows volatility and sustained losses, warranting credit and contract-duration assessments. For organizations evaluating Lantronix partnerships, direct procurement discussions should address security certifications, CISO/PSIRT availability, SOM availability, and regulatory compliance roadmaps against the CRA/NIS2/GDPR timeline.
