## 5. Regulatory Exposure and Compliance Landscape

### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Article 3(1) Classification

**Status:** Not publicly disclosed by Wärtsilä.

The **EU Cyber Resilience Act (2024)** requires manufacturers of "products with digital elements" to conduct conformity assessments and ensure compliance with essential cybersecurity requirements (Article 3). Products are classified as **Article 7 Class I** (lower risk; self-assessment permitted) or **Article 7 Class II** (higher risk; third-party notified body assessment required). [[31]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act)

**Assessment:** Wärtsilä's marine engines (Wärtsilä 31, 46F, etc.) incorporate embedded digital controls (UNIC/CCM), making them plausibly within CRA Article 3(1) scope. Similarly, GridSolve BESS, GEMS software, and navigation systems contain significant digital elements. However, **no Wärtsilä public documentation confirms Article 3(1) applicability or Article 7 Class I vs. Class II classification**. The company may have determined exemptions based on (1) product lifecycle rules (engines produced pre-CRA effective date may have transitional relief), (2) ODM arrangements (battery supplier RCT Power may carry responsibility for certain digital components), or (3) industrial exception carve-outs. **CRA classification requires direct engagement with Wärtsilä's legal/compliance teams.**

### EU NIS2 Directive Applicability

**Status:** Uncertain; likely applies to certain Wärtsilä business units.

The **NIS2 Directive (Directive 2024/2555)** establishes cybersecurity requirements for "critical sectors" (energy, transport, water, health, finance, digital infrastructure, space). Operators of critical infrastructure within those sectors must implement NIS2-mandated security measures and incident reporting protocols.
