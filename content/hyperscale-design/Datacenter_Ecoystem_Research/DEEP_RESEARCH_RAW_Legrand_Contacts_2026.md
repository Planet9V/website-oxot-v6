### Named Cybersecurity & IT Leadership

### Product Security, PSIRT, and CISO Leadership: Not Publicly Disclosed

Despite comprehensive research across multiple channels—company investor relations pages, annual regulatory filings (Universal Registration Document), Raritan corporate leadership pages, LinkedIn corporate profiles, industry conference directories (RSAC, Black Hat, InfoSec), and security community resources—**no specific CISO, Chief Product Security Officer, or PSIRT Director names for Legrand SA or its Raritan division are publicly documented** [[26]](https://www.legrand.com/en/investors-and-shareholders/annual-report-and-registration-document/2024) [[28]](https://www.legrand.com/sites/default/files/Documents_PDF_Legrand/Finance/2026/autre/DUE/Legrand_URD_2025_ENGLISH%201_1776154094.pdf) [[29]](https://www.raritan.com/about-us/newsroom) [[36]](https://www.legrand.us/about-us/leadership).

This absence does not indicate a lack of governance; rather, it reflects a common organizational pattern in which security functions are managed at divisional or functional levels without corresponding public-facing executive officer disclosures. The **Universal Registration Document 2025** (the official regulatory filing equivalent to a 10-K in the US) explicitly documents that Legrand maintains **centralized IT management with structured incident handling (CERT/SOC capabilities)**, follows NIST-based IT risk mapping frameworks, and implements ISO27001-certified security processes [[28]](https://www.legrand.com/sites/default/files/Documents_PDF_Legrand/Finance/2026/autre/DUE/Legrand_URD_2025_ENGLISH%201_1776154094.pdf). However, specific names and titles for these functions are not disclosed in public documents.

### Implications for Leadership Identification

**Organizational structure:** Legrand's cybersecurity governance appears to be managed through:
- Centralized IT leadership (under CIO Julianne LeBlanc)
- Divisional product security (Raritan embedded within Legrand's broader digital infrastructure group)
- Functional security teams (CERT/SOC) reporting through IT hierarchy rather than as independent CISO-level functions

**Industry context:** This governance model—where security functions do not have independent executive officer visibility—is not unusual among European diversified manufacturers. Companies organizing by division (Legrand's structure: Electrical Infrastructure, Digital Infrastructure, which contains Raritan) often manage security as a functional responsibility within each division rather than as a separate C-suite reporting line.

**Conference visibility:** A search of 2024-2026 cybersecurity conference speaker directories including RSAC 2026, Black Hat, Infosec, and CISO-specific events (CISO Singapore, CISO Malaysia, CISO Visions) yielded no confirmed speaking engagements or panel participation by named Legrand/Raritan security personnel [[10]](https://asimily.com/blog/top-ten-cybersecurity-conferences/) [[11]](https://www.techtarget.com/searchsecurity/feature/Cybersecurity-conferences-to-attend) [[12]](https://infosec-conferences.com/black-hat/) [[13]](https://www.rsa.com/rsac/) [[14]](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/cybersecurity-infosec-conferences-events.html) [[15]](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/rsa-conference.html) [[16]](https://www.infosecinstitute.com/events/rsac-conference/) [[17]](https://www.rsaconference.com/usa) [[18]](https://nordvpn.com/cybersecurity/events/).

## 4. EU Regulatory Risk Disclosures: NIS2, GDPR, and Critical Infrastructure Obligations

### NIS2 Directive Applicability and Timeline

The **NIS2 Directive** (Network and Information Systems Security Directive 2) represents the primary regulatory framework affecting Legrand's digital infrastructure business in the European Union [[20]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive) [[22]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/) [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond). Key requirements:

| Requirement | Timeline | Impact |
|-------------|----------|--------|
| **Transposition into national law** | **October 24, 2024** | **All EU member states required** [[20]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive) [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond) |
| **Registration with national authorities** | **December 1, 2024 onward** | **Data centers (Annex I) and digital service providers (Annex II) must register** [[8]](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=fa165452-cf1a-4817-b486-e0a2e3716ccb) |
| **Risk assessments (mandatory)** | **Ongoing** | **Organizations must conduct and document cybersecurity risk management measures** [[27]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/Mapping%20NIS%202%20obligations%20with%20ECSF%20role%20profiles.pdf) |
| **Incident reporting (critical)** | **24-48 hours to authorities** | **Mandatory notification of significant incidents** [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond) |
| **Board-level accountability** | **Governance requirement** | **CISO or equivalent must report to board** [[27]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/Mapping%20NIS%202%20obligations%20with%20ECSF%20role%20profiles.pdf) |

Sources: [[8]](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=fa165452-cf1a-4817-b486-e0a2e3716ccb) [[19]](https://nflo.tech/knowledge-base/cyber-security-landscape-2024-2025-global-and-regional-cyber-security-regulations/) [[20]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive) [[21]](https://hyperproof.io/nis2/) [[22]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/) [[23]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/nis2-technical-implementation-guidance) [[24]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ENISA_Technical_implementation_guidance_on_cybersecurity_risk_management_measures_version_1.0.pdf) [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond) [[27]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/Mapping%20NIS%202%20obligations%20with%20ECSF%20role%20profiles.pdf)

## 8. Strategic Assessment: Governance Maturity vs. Leadership Transparency

### Governance Maturity: What's Documented

Legrand demonstrates **enterprise-grade cybersecurity governance** as evidenced by:

1. **Formal certifications**: ISO27001 (April 2024), NIAP 4.0
2. **Third-party validation**: VAPT testing, ESG ratings (AA MSCI, Platinum Ecovadis)

5. **Policy frameworks**: Privacy by Design, Security by Design, risk management measures
6. **Board-level attention**: Cybersecurity identified as material operational risk in regulatory filings

### Leadership Transparency Gap: What's Not Disclosed

The organization does **not publicly disclose**:
- Named Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
- Named Chief Product Security Officer
- Named PSIRT (Product Security Incident Response Team) Director
- LinkedIn profiles for dedicated security leaders
- Conference presentations or speaking engagements
- Published security research or threat analysis reports

### Organizational Implications

This transparency gap is consistent with **two organizational patterns**:

**Pattern 1: Divisional/Functional Security Model**
- Security functions report through CIO (Julianne LeBlanc) rather than as independent C-suite position
- Security is embedded within IT operations (CERT/SOC) rather than as separate office
- PSIRT is managed as a technical function rather than as branded public-facing program
- This is common among European industrial/infrastructure companies

**Pattern 2: B2B vs. B2C Risk Profile**
- Raritan's customers (Fortune 500 data centers, critical infrastructure operators) negotiate security requirements through procurement rather than relying on public CSO visibility
- Security certifications (ISO27001, NIAP, VAPT) are primary trust signals rather than named executives
- Conference visibility is less critical when customers conduct direct security assessments

1. **Data breach risk**: Exposure of customer data or intellectual property (mitigated by GDPR framework, encryption, incident response)
2. **Operational disruption**: Ransomware or DDoS affecting manufacturing/logistics (mitigated by CERT/SOC, disaster recovery procedures)
3. **Product tampering**: Unauthorized firmware or hardware modifications (mitigated by secure boot, supply chain controls)
4. **Supply chain compromise**: Third-party vulnerabilities affecting Legrand's products (mitigated by vendor risk assessments, noted in URD)
5. **Regulatory non-compliance**: Failure to meet NIS2, GDPR, or critical infrastructure standards (mitigated by ISO27001, governance structures, training programs)

### NIS2 Compliance Timeline & Urgency

The **October 2024 NIS2 Directive transposition deadline** has passed, and the **December 1, 2024 registration deadline** for data center essential entities has also passed [[8]](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=fa165452-cf1a-4817-b486-e0a2e3716ccb). Organizations not yet in formal compliance are now operating in a **non-compliance status** that creates:

- **Regulatory exposure**: Penalties up to **€10 million or 2% of global revenue** (whichever is higher) under GDPR Article 83(5) [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond)
- **Operational risk**: Mandatory incident reporting (24-48 hour window) for incidents affecting critical infrastructure [[25]](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-nis2-and-beyond)
- **Board accountability**: NIS2 mandates that cybersecurity governance is elevated to board/executive committee level [[27]](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/Mapping%20NIS%202%20obligations%20with%20ECSF%20role%20profiles.pdf)

Legrand's formal registration with national authorities and board-level CISO accountability structures are now **critical operational requirements** rather than optional governance enhancements.

### Recommendations for Investors & Partners

1. **Request formal disclosure** of NIS2 compliance certification status in next annual report/URD
2. **Seek confirmation** of board-level CISO or equivalent security governance role (either new hire or CIO elevation)
3. **Monitor announcement** of senior security leadership appointments (likely 2026 given NIS2 timeline)
4. **Request SLAs** on PSIRT vulnerability response times and disclosure policies (currently not publicly documented)
5. **Evaluate supply chain security assessments** performed on Raritan subcomponents and third-party dependencies

## 10. Conclusion: Financial Strength with Governance Maturity Questions

Legrand SA demonstrates **strong financial performance** (€9.5B revenue, 9.6% growth, 20.7% margins) with a **rapidly expanding Digital Infrastructure segment** (26% of revenue, 28% annual growth) that creates both opportunity and regulatory obligation. The company has implemented **enterprise-grade cybersecurity infrastructure** (ISO27001, NIAP, VAPT testing) and committed to **ambitious ESG cybersecurity goals** (Privacy by Design, Security by Design, 19,000 employees trained, >25% R&D in software).

However, the **absence of publicly named CISO, Chief Product Security Officer, or PSIRT Director positions** creates a transparency gap that is incongruous with the company's material cybersecurity risks and NIS2 regulatory obligations. Under NIS2, cybersecurity governance at the board level is **now a legal requirement**, not a governance best practice. The next **12-18 months (2026 calendar year)** will likely see significant changes in Legrand's organizational structure to formalize security leadership in response to NIS2 compliance deadlines and investor pressure for governance transparency.

For stakeholders evaluating Legrand or Raritan division opportunities, **the governance maturity is evident in infrastructure and certifications, but leadership visibility remains a development opportunity** that should be explicitly addressed in vendor negotiations or investment theses.
