**Security Program Profile:** Cummins does not hold formal product-level cybersecurity certifications such as IEC 62443, ISO 27001, or SOC 2. The company manages product security under internal quality control systems without independent third-party validation, creating verification challenges for data center buyers.

### 3.2 EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Exposure
Cummins' generator controllers and software qualify as products with digital elements:
*   **PowerCommand Controllers:** Class II (Important) products due to their operational role in backup power systems for critical infrastructure, requiring third-party security audits.
*   **Accelera and PowerCommand Software:** Class I or II depending on remote connectivity and virtual machine installations.

**Penalty Exposure:** Non-compliance with CRA rules exposes Cummins to fines up to 2.5% of global annual turnover, representing a maximum potential fine of approximately $850 million based on FY2024 revenue.

### 3.3 NIS2 & US Infrastructure Regulations
*   **NIS2 Directive:** Affects Cummins through its European utility and data center customers who must verify that backup generator suppliers maintain secure development processes and component supply chains.
*   **NERC CIP:** Applies to Cummins PowerCommand systems sold to North American bulk electric system operators. The company lacks publicly documented NERC CIP hardening guides, requiring utility operators to perform custom compliance mapping.

### 3.4 Publicly Disclosed Vulnerabilities (Last 24 Months)
Cummins does not run a public product security incident response team (PSIRT). Vulnerability reporting and patching are managed internally through customer support channels.
*   **Vulnerability Disclosures:** No public CVEs or CISA security advisories have been published for Cummins PowerCommand systems in the past 24 months.
