**Article 7 Classification — CRA Class I vs. Class II:**

The CRA distinguishes between **Class I ("basic cybersecurity requirements")** and **Class II ("enhanced cybersecurity requirements")**. Classification depends on **systemic risk** if the product fails:

- **Class I:** Products with lower systemic risk (e.g., simple IoT sensors, low-bandwidth devices, limited connectivity)
- **Class II:** Products with high systemic risk if compromised (e.g., network devices, security appliances, critical infrastructure connectivity, products that handle sensitive data)

### Assessment of Data Gap

**Why No Public CVEs Despite 7,263 Employees and EUR 1.7B Revenue?**

**Most Likely Explanation: Defense/Critical Infrastructure Customer Base**

The absence of public CVEs is **consistent with** (not contradictory to) a strong security posture, and reflects **deliberate business model choices**:

1. **Defense Contractor Confidentiality:** Customers with classified contracts (U.S. DoD, NATO, etc.) often require **non-disclosure of vulnerabilities and patches** to avoid revealing system capabilities or attack surfaces. Kontron's EUR 165M defense order and ITAR certification align with this requirement.

2. **Critical Infrastructure Operator Preference:** Utilities (grid operators, railway authorities) prefer **private vendor notification** rather than public CVE announcement; public disclosure creates operational risk during patch deployment windows.

3. **Extended Embargo Periods:** Kontron likely practices 90-180 day vulnerability embargo periods (or longer for defense), delaying public disclosure while customers patch.

4. **Lack of Consumer-Facing Products:** Unlike Cisco, HP, or Microsoft (which have vast consumer IoT/network products with high public vulnerability disclosure rates), Kontron focuses on **B2B industrial hardware** where each customer is a known entity with contractual support agreements.

5. **Strategic Security Positioning:** Kontron's marketing emphasizes "European, trustworthy, secure-by-design" differentiation. Aggressive public disclosure of CVEs would undermine this narrative (competitor intelligence concern).

**Conclusion:** The **absence of public CVEs is a feature, not a bug**, for Kontron's target customer segments. Enterprise customers in transportation, aerospace, and defense explicitly value **vendor confidentiality and private patch channels** over public transparency.

- **Q3-Q4 2026:** Shareholder vote (likely approval given Ennoconn's board majority)
- **Q1 2027:** Acquisition close (typical post-antitrust clearance)

**Implications for B2B Buyers:**
- ⚠ **Organizational Changes:** Post-acquisition, Kontron likely becomes Foxconn's "European embedded computing division" with potential restructuring of sales teams, technical support, and R&D priorities
- ⚠ **Supply Chain Shift:** Manufacturing could migrate toward Foxconn/Ennoconn facilities (Taiwan, Vietnam, China) away from German/European operations, contradicting current CRA supply-chain-resilience positioning
- ✓ **Positive Scenarios:** Foxconn's 500K+ suppliers could accelerate component availability; manufacturing cost reduction could enable aggressive pricing
- **Recommendation:** Enterprise customers should negotiate **multi-year framework agreements** with change-of-control protections before Q3 2026 formal offer announcement to lock in pricing, support commitments, and product roadmap certainty
