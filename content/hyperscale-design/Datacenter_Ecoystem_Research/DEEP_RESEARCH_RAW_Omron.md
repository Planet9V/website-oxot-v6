## 7. Regulatory Exposure & Compliance Framework

### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Applicability

OMRON has **publicly acknowledged the applicability of the EU Cyber Resilience Act** and is **actively preparing for compliance**. However, **specific Article 3(1) scope determinations and Article 7 classifications (Class I vs. Class II "important products")** have **not been publicly disclosed**. [[10]](https://industrial.omron.eu/en/news-discover/news/omron-europe-achieves-iec-62443-4-1-certification) [[39]](https://www.ia.omron.com/product/iec62443-4-1/index.html)

**What This Means for B2B Procurement:**
- The CRA applies to "products with digital elements" placed on the EU market
- "Important products" (Article 7) include network services, firewalls, operating systems, and specific device categories (industrial control systems, network routers, etc.)
- "Critical products" (higher tier) include specified operating systems and industrial control systems
- OMRON controllers and connected industrial equipment likely fall under Article 7 scope, requiring:
  - Cybersecurity requirements throughout product lifecycle
  - Vulnerability disclosure timelines (60 days for critical vulnerabilities)
  - Security incident reporting obligations
  - Coordinated vulnerability disclosure processes

### NIS2 Directive & Critical Infrastructure

OMRON acknowledges NIS2 Directive applicability and is **in active preparation phase** for compliance. The NIS2 Directive extends mandatory cybersecurity requirements to operators of essential services (energy, transport, water, health) and important digital service providers. OMRON products serving critical infrastructure operators will be affected downstream, though OMRON itself may not be directly regulated unless it qualifies as an essential service operator or important digital service provider. [[10]](https://industrial.omron.eu/en/news-discover/news/omron-europe-achieves-iec-62443-4-1-certification) [[39]](https://www.ia.omron.com/product/iec62443-4-1/index.html)

### China Cybersecurity Law

OMRON explicitly acknowledges the **applicability of China's Cybersecurity Law** for products sold and services rendered within China, particularly for data processing and network security requirements. [[39]](https://www.ia.omron.com/product/iec62443-4-1/index.html)

### Data Gap: NERC CIP & NRC 10 CFR 73.54

**No evidence exists that OMRON qualifies as a regulated entity under:**
- **NERC CIP (Critical Infrastructure Protection) Standards:** These apply to owners and operators of the North American Electric Reliability Corporation bulk power system. No public disclosure indicates OMRON operates such infrastructure.
- **NRC 10 CFR Part 73.54 (Cybersecurity for Nuclear Power Reactors):** This applies to nuclear power reactor facilities. No indication exists that OMRON operates nuclear facilities.

However, **OMRON controllers and automation systems may be used WITHIN critical infrastructure and nuclear facilities**, making OMRON an upstream supplier to regulated entities. In such cases, OMRON's products must meet applicable security standards as specified by the customer.

### GDPR Data Processing

OMRON acknowledges GDPR applicability for European operations and customer data handling. Specific **data processing agreements, privacy impact assessments, and data retention policies are not publicly detailed** in available sources.

OMRON Corporation represents a **mature, globally diversified industrial automation manufacturer** with a **strong commitment to cybersecurity standards (IEC 62443-4-1 certified) and an active vulnerability disclosure program**.
