## 1. Company Overview and Organizational Structure

### Legal Identity and Founding History

**Rolls-Royce Power Systems AG** is the formal legal entity, renamed in January 2014 from its previous identity as **Tognum AG**. The company traces its operational heritage to **1909**, when Wilhelm and Karl Maybach founded Luftfahrzeug-Motorenbau GmbH, establishing more than a century of continuous engine and propulsion systems development [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Friedrichshafen) [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Power_Systems).

The organization's modern structure emerged through several transformations: Tognum AG acquired MTU Friedrichshafen (the primary operating subsidiary [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Friedrichshafen)) through strategic consolidation, and in 2011, Rolls-Royce Holdings plc and Daimler AG launched a joint **\$4.2 billion public tender offer** for 100% of Tognum's shares [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Holdings). This acquisition was completed in 2014, establishing Rolls-Royce Power Systems as a wholly-owned subsidiary [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Friedrichshafen). The 2019 rebranding initiative unified all MTU-branded entities under the consolidated "mtu" identity to improve market recognition [[8]](https://www.miningmagazine.com/operations/news/1368931/rolls-royce-rebrand-mtu-companies) [[4]](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/mtu-companies-rolling-into-the-rolls-royce-name/).

## 10. Security Incidents and Vulnerability History

### Publicly Disclosed Product Vulnerabilities

**CVE Search Results (36-Month Period: April 2023 - June 2026)**:

A comprehensive search of the National Vulnerability Database (NVD) [[33]](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2024-2026) [[34]](https://nvd.nist.gov/) [[35]](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2024-2025) [[36]](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-2474) and CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog [[17]](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) yields **zero CVEs** attributed to:
- Rolls-Royce Power Systems AG
- MTU (product brand)
- MTU Friedrichshafen
- Specific MTU product models (Series 4000, Series 2000, Series 1600, Bergen Engines)

**Data Interpretation**:

1. **Product Lifecycle Characteristics**: Industrial generator sets have 20-30+ year operational lifecycles with infrequent firmware updates and changes. This limits the exposure window and research incentive compared to rapidly-updated IT software.

2. **Operational Environment**: MTU generator sets typically operate in:
   - Air-gapped (disconnected) industrial environments
   - Restricted-access facilities (data centers, utilities)
   - Legacy networks without internet connectivity
   
   This reduces the probability of external exploitation and public disclosure.

3. **Responsible Disclosure Preferred**: If vulnerabilities are discovered, researchers and customers are likely to report them through the formal **responsible.disclosure@rolls-royce.com** channel [[26]](https://www.rolls-royce.com/~/media/Files/R/Rolls-Royce/documents/contact-us/vulnerability-disclosure-policy-2020.pdf) [[27]](https://www.rolls-royce.com/~/media/Files/R/Rolls-Royce/documents/contact-us/vulnerability-disclosure-policy.pdf) rather than publicly disclose in NVD.

4. **Industry Norm**: Critical infrastructure OT products (power generation, transmission, distribution) rarely appear in public CVE databases. Security practices in OT environments emphasize confidentiality around vulnerabilities to prevent public awareness of attack vectors in deployed systems.

### PSIRT/Incident Response Capability

**Formal Vulnerability Disclosure Program** [[26]](https://www.rolls-royce.com/~/media/Files/R/Rolls-Royce/documents/contact-us/vulnerability-disclosure-policy-2020.pdf) [[27]](https://www.rolls-royce.com/~/media/Files/R/Rolls-Royce/documents/contact-us/vulnerability-disclosure-policy.pdf):

- Dedicated email address for reports: responsible.disclosure@rolls-royce.com
- PGP encryption key provided for confidential communications
- Policy commitments to:
  - Acknowledge receipt of vulnerability reports
  - Work with researchers on responsible disclosure timelines
  - Credit researchers in security advisories (when applicable)

**Incident Response Readiness**: While no formal "PSIRT" designation appears in public organizational charts, the published vulnerability disclosure policy and corporate security governance demonstrate incident response capability. Rolls-Royce Power Systems' parent company (Rolls-Royce Holdings plc) maintains enterprise-level security operations, with security incident response cascading to operating divisions.

**Assessment**: Rolls-Royce Power Systems demonstrates mature security governance aligned with critical infrastructure norms. The absence of public CVEs reflects both strong security posture and the operational technology characteristics of industrial power generation equipment.
