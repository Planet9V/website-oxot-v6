### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Applicability

The **EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation EU 2024/2847)**, effective September 2025 with compliance deadlines September 2026 (Important products) and December 2027 (Critical products), creates cascading compliance obligations for industrial automation manufacturers [[42]](https://zealience.com/resource-hub/cyber-resilience-act-guide-manufacturers/) [[43]](https://ipc2u.com/articles/knowledge-base/industrial-cybersecurity-readiness-in-the-eu-a-practical-guide-to-the-cyber-resilience-act-iec-62443/).

**Article 3(1) "Products with Digital Elements" Scope:** B&R automation products incorporating POWERLINK, OPC UA, Automation Runtime, and industrial PCs inherently satisfy the CRA definition of "products with digital elements" (products with at least one hardware component capable of executing software) [[37]](https://www.windriver.com/sites/default/files/2024-12/658750_EU%20Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20FAQ.pdf) [[39]](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/events/2025/2025-09-09-en-iec-62443-to-cra/) [[40]](https://zealience.com/resource-hub/cyber-resilience-act-product-categories/). **Specific Article 3(1) determination for B&R's full portfolio not publicly disclosed.** B&R has signaled compliance intent through publication of POWERLINK CRA alignment guide and TÜV Rheinland engagement [[6]](https://www.br-automation.com/en/service/cyber-security/).

**Article 7 Classification (Class I vs. Class II):** CRA mandates classification of products into Default, Important (Class I), or Critical (Class II) categories based on essential functions and impact severity [[40]](https://zealience.com/resource-hub/cyber-resilience-act-product-categories/). Industrial automation controllers (PLCs, motion controls, industrial PCs) serving critical infrastructure potentially qualify as **Important or Critical products**, subjecting them to heightened cybersecurity, security update, and transparency requirements. **B&R has not publicly declared its CRA Article 7 classification for specific products.** Inference: POWERLINK-based products likely Class I (Important) given TÜV Rheinland involvement; consumer-facing vision systems likely Default [[38]](https://cyberstand.eu/events/cra-standards-unlocked-en-iec-62443-cra-ot-cybersecurity-important-products-class-i-ii) [[39]](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/events/2025/2025-09-09-en-iec-62443-to-cra/) [[42]](https://zealience.com/resource-hub/cyber-resilience-act-guide-manufacturers/).

**B&R's CRA Response Posture:** Proactive. Company published dedicated POWERLINK CRA compliance guide and engaged TÜV Rheinland for third-party verification [[6]](https://www.br-automation.com/en/service/cyber-security/). IEC 62443-4-1 certification demonstrates organizational maturity for translating CRA security requirements into engineering practice [[41]](https://www.exida.com/blog/how-iec-62443-can-help-achieve-compliance-with-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act-cra) [[42]](https://zealience.com/resource-hub/cyber-resilience-act-guide-manufacturers/).

### NIS2 Directive Applicability

The EU Network and Information Systems Directive (NIS2, Directive 2022/2555/EU), effective October 2024, imposes mandatory cybersecurity requirements on "operators of essential services" and "digital service providers" in critical sectors (energy, water, transport, health, banking, DNS/TLD operators, cloud/data center providers, managed security service providers) [[43]](https://ipc2u.com/articles/knowledge-base/industrial-cybersecurity-readiness-in-the-eu-a-practical-guide-to-the-cyber-resilience-act-iec-62443/).

**B&R's NIS2 Exposure:** **Applicability not addressed in available sources.** B&R as an industrial automation vendor (Tier 2 supplier) would be subject to NIS2 **supply chain security obligations** (Directive Article 21, "Supply chain security") if direct customers operate essential services (e.g., B&R supplies motion control to an oil refinery). B&R itself would not be an "operator of essential services" unless it directly operates critical infrastructure. **Recommendation: Assess on a customer-by-customer basis whether end-user is subject to NIS2; if so, B&R products must conform to NIS2's "security by design and default" mandate.** No evidence of formal NIS2 alignment program in available search results.

#### **CVE-2025-11044** — Automation Runtime

- **CVSS v3.1 Score:** **6.8 (Medium-High)** [[22]](https://www.isssource.com/update-for-abb-br-automation-runtime/) [[23]](https://www.dataproof.co.za/index.php/2026/05/05/abb-br-automation-runtime/) [[25]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-125-03)
- **Vulnerability Type:** CWE-119: Improper Restriction of Operations within Bounds of Memory Buffer
- **Root Cause:** Race condition in Automation Runtime task scheduler causing resource exhaustion (DoS condition)
- **Attack Vector:** Remote network-based; unauthenticated; exploitable via rapid packet transmission inducing race condition
- **Impact:** Denial of Service — Runtime process crashes, halting machine automation
- **Affected Versions:** Automation Runtime <6.5, Runtime R4.93 and earlier (<R4.93)
- **Fixed Versions:** 6.5+, R4.93 and later
- **Mitigations:**

  - Implement network traffic limiting via firewall (max 80% link utilization recommended)
  - Deploy network-level DoS protection (rate limiting, SYN flood filtering)
- **CISA Publication:** January 19, 2026 (ICSA-26-125-03)
- **B&R PSIRT Advisory:** SA25P005 (January 19, 2026) — "ANSL Server flooding/DoS" [[25]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-125-03)

#### **CVE-2025-11043** — Automation Studio

- **CISA Publication:** January 19, 2026 (ICSA-26-125-04)
- **B&R PSIRT Advisory:** SA25P004 (January 19, 2026) — "Automation Studio certificate validation" [[26]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-125-04)

### UEFI Firmware Vulnerabilities (Multiple Industrial PC Models)

#### **CVE-2023-45229 through CVE-2023-45237** — UEFI Preboot Execution Environment (PXE) Stack

- **CVSS v3.1 Scores:** **8.3 (High)** to 7.5 (High) [[29]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-141-02)
- **Vulnerability Types:** CWE-125 (Out-of-bounds Read), CWE-119 (Buffer Overflow), CWE-835 (Infinite Loop), CWE-338 (Use of Cryptographically Weak Pseudo-Random Number Generator)
- **Root Cause:** Vulnerable EDK2 UEFI firmware stack used in B&R industrial PC boot environments; multiple memory safety and cryptographic weaknesses in PXE protocol implementation
- **Attack Vectors:**
  - Remote code execution via malformed PXE packets (network-based, no authentication)
  - DNS cache poisoning via weak PRNG in DNS resolver
  - Denial of service via infinite loop conditions
- **Impact:**
  - Remote code execution at pre-boot level (firmware execution privilege)
  - Machine takeover before operating system loads
  - DNS spoofing enabling MITM attacks on all network communications
- **Affected Industrial PC Models:**
  - **APC4100** (Firmware versions <1.09)
  - **APC910** (Firmware version 1.x) — **NO PATCH AVAILABLE; MITIGATION ONLY**
  - **C80** (Firmware <1.14)
  - **MPC3100** (unspecified versions)
  - **PPC1200** (unspecified versions)
  - **PPC900** (Firmware <2.16)
  - **APC2200** (unspecified)
  - **PPC2200** (unspecified)
  - **APC3100** (unspecified)
  - **PPC3100** (unspecified)
- **Fixed/Patched Versions:** Model-specific (e.g., APC910 1.x → no fix)
- **Critical Mitigation Requirement:**
  - **Disable PXE boot** in UEFI settings (eliminates attack surface)
  - Restrict network access to industrial PCs to trusted engineering/control networks only
  - Deploy network-level segmentation (physical or VLAN isolation)
  - Monitor for suspicious pre-boot activity (firmware logs, IPMI/Redfish telemetry if available)
- **CISA Publication:** May 21, 2026 (ICSA-26-141-02)
- **Special Note:** These vulnerabilities originate in **third-party EDK2 UEFI firmware stack** used by B&R but not developed by B&R; however, B&R bears responsibility for firmware integration, patching, and customer communication

## STRATEGIC INSIGHTS FOR B2B SALES INTELLIGENCE

1. **Market Position:** B&R holds differentiated position as only vertically integrated provider of robotics + PLC/motion control + real-time software stack with open-architecture positioning. This contrasts with competitors offering partial stacks (e.g., Siemens: motion but not robotics; Rockwell: control but not robots).

6. **Supply Chain Vulnerability:** Dependence on third-party EDK2 UEFI firmware creates inherited vulnerability exposure; any future UEFI CVEs will cascade into B&R PCs without B&R control over discovery timeline.
