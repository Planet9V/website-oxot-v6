## 6. Regulatory Exposure: EU Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2, and Sector-Specific Requirements

### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) – Applicability Framework

The European Commission's revised Cybersecurity Act, proposed January 20, 2026, introduces mandatory security requirements for "products with digital elements" sold in the EU [[27]](https://industrialcyber.co/regulation-standards-and-compliance/european-commission-proposes-revised-cybersecurity-act-to-boost-eu-cyber-resilience-secure-ict-supply-chains/) [[30]](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/european-commission-proposes-major-cybersecurity-package-to-strengthen-eu-cyber-resilience) [[31]](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/european-commission-announces-new-cybersecurity-package). The regulation establishes two product classes with differential compliance requirements [[32]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act):

- **Class I (Lower-Risk)**: Lower complexity products; faster conformity assessment (4 months)
- **Class II (Higher-Risk)**: Higher complexity, critical functionality, or supply chain risk products; longer assessment (6–9 months)

**Compliance Timeline**: Reporting begins September 11, 2026; compliance deadline December 11, 2027 [[28]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[29]](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mnj2/eu-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-for-2026-and-beyond/). **Penalty Structure**: Administrative fines up to \$15 million or 2.5% of annual global turnover [[29]](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102mnj2/eu-cybersecurity-regulatory-update-for-2026-and-beyond/) [[30]](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/european-commission-proposes-major-cybersecurity-package-to-strengthen-eu-cyber-resilience).

**Critical Assessment Gap**: Cummins has not publicly disclosed which products fall within Article 3(1) "products with digital elements" scope or their CRA Class I/II classifications. Products with networked ECUs, control systems, telematics, or predictive maintenance capabilities likely trigger scope, but Cummins-specific determinations are not disclosed. Direct engagement with EU regulatory or sales teams is recommended for accurate classification.

### NIS2 Directive – Critical Infrastructure Operator Requirements

The NIS2 Directive (Network and Information Systems Security 2) entered into force January 16, 2023, and designated October 18, 2026, as the compliance deadline for "essential entities" [[28]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[34]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/). Essential entity classification applies to operators of critical infrastructure in energy, transport, water, healthcare, digital infrastructure, and public administration sectors [[34]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/).

**Cummins Applicability**: As a manufacturer of power generation systems, engines, and drivetrain components serving critical infrastructure operators, Cummins may qualify as a **component supplier to essential entities** subject to supply chain security requirements under Article 19(1)(c) [[28]](https://hyperproof.io/understanding-the-relationship-between-nis2-and-the-eu-cyber-resilience-act/) [[33]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cybersecurity-policies).

**Regulatory Requirements** (if essential entity status triggered):
- 24-hour incident reporting to national competent authority [[34]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/)
- Risk management programs including threat modeling and supply chain vetting [[33]](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cybersecurity-policies)
- Supply chain security assessments for critical component suppliers [[34]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/)
- Administrative fines up to \$10 million or 2% of global turnover for essential entities [[34]](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/)

**Assessment Gap**: Cummins has not publicly disclosed whether it qualifies as an essential entity under NIS2 or implemented formal NIS2 supply chain security programs. This determination requires engagement with EU regulatory or compliance teams.

## 10. Strategic Positioning, Key Risks, and Competitive Implications

### Strategic Transformation to Zero-Emissions Powertrains

Cummins is executing a deliberate technology pivot from fossil-fuel combustion engines toward zero-emissions platforms [[14]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26172/000002617225000007/0000026172-25-000007.txt) [[23]](https://investor.cummins.com/company-info/leadership-team):

**Current Portfolio Transition**:
- **Legacy Diesel**: Remains majority revenue (>70% of Engine segment) but declining long-term as truck OEMs electrify product lines
- **Natural Gas**: B6.7N, L9N, X15N variants represent growth segment for applications requiring proven fuel infrastructure and extended range
- **Electric & Fuel Cell**: Accelera business targeting \$1 billion revenue run-rate by 2030 (currently \$369M FY2024, representing 3x growth trajectory)
- **Hydrogen**: Long-term development platform for Class 8 trucks requiring extended range and rapid refueling

**R&D Investment Reallocation**: The company maintains 4.3% of revenue (\$1.463 billion) in R&D with increasing allocation to electrification, battery systems, fuel cell stack development, and software/controls integration [[14]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26172/000002617225000007/0000026172-25-000007.txt). This spending level positions Cummins for capability buildout but lags full-cycle vehicle OEM R&D intensity (typically 5–8%), indicating reliance on OEM partners for vehicle-level integration and market development.

### Competitive Advantages and Market Position

1. **Integrated Powertrain Systems Capability**: Post-Meritor acquisition, Cummins offers engine + drivetrain + braking + axle + suspension integration—a rare capability among non-vertically-integrated OEMs. Competitors (Volvo Group, Daimler) are primarily truck manufacturers with internal powertrain divisions; Cummins remains the only major independent supplier offering complete powertrain systems across multiple OEMs.

2. **Global Supply Chain and Distributor Network**: 650 distributor locations and 19,000+ certified dealers across 190 countries provide unmatched aftermarket service infrastructure and spare parts availability—a critical competitive advantage in heavy-duty industries where vehicle downtime is costly.

3. **Zero-Emissions Partnerships**: Joint ownership in Amplify Cell Technologies alongside PACCAR and Daimler positions Cummins as a trusted partner in battery electrification with direct cost advantages versus purchasing from Chinese suppliers.

4. **China Market Access**: Three 50%-owned JVs with combined equity income >$395M provide embedded positions in the world's largest commercial vehicle market and exposure to Chinese government industrial policy support for electrification.

### Key Business Risks

1. **OEM Customer Concentration**: Four OEMs account for the vast majority of engine and drivetrain revenue. Loss of primary position with any OEM or OEM financial distress (similar to Daimler's 2019 restructuring) could materially impact revenue [[14]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26172/000002617225000007/0000026172-25-000007.txt).

2. **Technology Transition Execution**: Accelera growing at 3x pace but remains <2% of total revenue. Success requires: (a) rapid OEM adoption of electric powertrains, (b) battery cost parity with diesel equivalent, (c) charging infrastructure development beyond Cummins' control, (d) successful Amplify Cell Technologies ramp to 2027 production.

3. **China Geopolitical Risk**: >40% of equity income from three China JVs exposes the company to Chinese regulatory changes, joint venture partner pressure, technology transfer demands, or trade sanctions impacting commercial vehicle exports to China [[14]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26172/000002617225000007/0000026172-25-000007.txt).

4. **Regulatory Compliance and Settlement History**: The December 2023 EPA/CARB settlement (\$2 billion charge, April 2024 payment) for emissions defeat devices on Dodge Ram engines signals risk of regulatory enforcement relating to engine control software. Future regulatory actions on particulate matter (PM), nitrogen oxides (NOx), or other pollutants could trigger similar charges.

5. **Cybersecurity Disclosure Gaps**: Absence of public PSIRT program, CVE disclosure database, or formal product security certification creates perception risk with large enterprise/government customers evaluating vendors for critical infrastructure applications. Organizations requiring formal security certifications may perceive Cummins as higher-risk versus competitors with published cybersecurity programs.

Cummins Inc. is a diversified, global powertrain leader with \$34.1 billion in revenue, 69,600 employees, and dominant market positions in heavy-duty commercial vehicles, power generation, and industrial equipment. The company's acquisition of Meritor transformed it into a rare integrated systems supplier capable of offering engine + drivetrain + braking solutions to truck OEMs globally. Strategic investments in Accelera and joint ownership of Amplify Cell Technologies position Cummins for growth in zero-emissions markets through 2030.

- Proven long-term OEM relationships (PACCAR, Daimler, Traton, Stellantis) with multi-decade supply agreements
- Global distributor network (650+ locations, 19,000+ dealers) enabling rapid market penetration
- Integrated powertrain systems capability post-Meritor (rare competitive advantage)
- Substantial R&D investment (\$1.463B FY2024) signaling commitment to emerging technologies

- Product-level security documentation (SBOM, firmware update procedures, vulnerability disclosure history) – request from sales engineering
- Customer case studies in regulated industries (energy, critical infrastructure) – request from market development

For B2B sales organizations targeting utilities, critical infrastructure operators, or government agencies, direct engagement with Cummins' Chief Information Security Officer, Principal Engineer – Product Cybersecurity, and compliance teams is essential to validate security posture and regulatory alignment prior to contract negotiations.
