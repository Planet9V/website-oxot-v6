## 10. INCIDENTS AND VULNERABILITIES – COMPREHENSIVE DISCLOSURE SUMMARY

Mitsubishi Electric has transparently disclosed **eight material security vulnerabilities** affecting its industrial control, HVAC, automation software, and robotics product lines in the past 24–36 months. Each CVE has been published through the **U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)** with detailed mitigation guidance and patch release timelines.

### CVE Severity and Product Impact Summary

**Highest Severity Incidents:**

1. **CVE-2025-3699 (CVSS 9.8 – Critical):** Authentication bypass vulnerability in **Mitsubishi Electric Air Conditioning Systems** enables remote attackers to bypass authentication and directly control AC units without valid credentials [[20]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-177-01) [[22]](https://cyberpress.org/critical-vulnerability-in-mitsubishi-electric/). Researcher **Mihály Csonka** identified the vulnerability; patch released June 26, 2025 [[22]](https://cyberpress.org/critical-vulnerability-in-mitsubishi-electric/).

2. **CVE-2025-15080 (CVSS 9.4 – Critical):** Information disclosure, information tampering, and denial-of-service vulnerabilities in **MELSEC iQ-R Series PLCs** affecting **Mitsubishi Electric proprietary protocol communication and SLMP (Seamless Message Protocol) communication** [[38]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-036-02). Disclosed February 5, 2026 [[38]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-26-036-02).

**High Severity Incidents (CVSS 8.0+):**

3. **CVE-2025-11774 (CVSS 8.2 – High):** OS command injection vulnerability in **ICONICS Digital Solutions and Mitsubishi Electric Products** disclosed December 31, 2025 [[19]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-352-04).

4. **CVE-2024-8299, CVE-2024-8300, CVE-2024-9852 (CVSS 7.8 – High):** Local code execution vulnerabilities in **ICONICS Digital Solutions** affecting multiple product versions; disclosed December 3, 2024 [[21]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-24-338-04).

5. **CVE-2024-11477 (CVSS 8.1 – High):** Integer underflow vulnerability in **MELSOFT Update Manager** affecting secure firmware update procedures; disclosed July 4, 2025 [[24]](https://industrialcyber.co/cisa/cisa-issues-ics-advisories-highlighting-hardware-flaws-in-hitachi-energy-mitsubishi-electric-industrial-systems/).

**Moderate to Medium Severity Incidents:**

6. **CVE-2025-0411 (CVSS 7.8 – High):** Protection mechanism failure in **MELSOFT Update Manager** [[24]](https://industrialcyber.co/cisa/cisa-issues-ics-advisories-highlighting-hardware-flaws-in-hitachi-energy-mitsubishi-electric-industrial-systems/).

7. **CVE-2024-7316 (CVSS 5.9 – Medium):** Denial-of-service vulnerability in **Mitsubishi Electric CNC Series** disclosed October 17, 2024 [[18]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-24-291-03).

8. **CVE-2025-0921 (CVSS 6.5 – Medium-High):** Privilege escalation vulnerability in **ICONICS Digital Solutions** disclosed May 20, 2025 [[23]](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-25-140-04).

## CONCLUSION

Mitsubishi Electric Corporation is a **mature, globally diversified industrial manufacturer** with established factory automation, robotics, power systems, and semiconductors business lines. The company demonstrates **increasing security maturity** through PSIRT establishment, CNA certification, FIRST membership, and transparent vulnerability disclosure. Recent strategic investments (**Nozomi Networks acquisition, Foxconn partnership, AWS strategic alignment**) signal commitment to next-generation OT/IoT security, mobility, and cloud-platform opportunities.

However, **material security risks** persist—particularly CVE-2025-3699 (CVSS 9.8) in air conditioning systems and recurring ICONICS SCADA platform vulnerabilities—requiring enterprise vigilance in patch management and vendor engagement. **Regulatory compliance maturity is strong** in EU CRA and PSIRT governance but **remains incomplete** in specific product IEC 62443 certifications and granular NIS2 applicability mapping.

Enterprises evaluating Mitsubishi Electric for critical-infrastructure or regulated-industry deployments should prioritize **direct vendor engagement** on IEC 62443 certifications, EU CRA product classifications, and NERC CIP compatibility to validate alignment with specific regulatory and operational requirements.
