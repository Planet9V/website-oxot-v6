### EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) — Products with Digital Elements

**Scope & Applicability:**

The **EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation 2024/2847)** establishes mandatory security requirements for "products with digital elements" distributed in the EU market. Article 3(1) defines in-scope products as those containing "software or other forms of digital technology" that can connect to the internet or network interfaces.

**Likely Scope Classification for Airedale Products:**
- ✓ **CDU10/12/15 (Coolant Distribution Units):** Likely in-scope
  - Reasoning: Contain embedded firmware, network interfaces (BACnet IP, Modbus), cloud connectivity (Diagnostics™), touchscreen HMI
  - Confirmation needed: Modine has not publicly confirmed Article 3(1) classification
  
- ✓ **CRAH Units (EdgeAire, SmartCool One):** Likely in-scope
  - Reasoning: Network-capable (BACnet IP, Modbus), integration with BMS systems
  
- ✓ **CRAC/Chiller Units with Network Controls:** Likely in-scope
  - Reasoning: Modular controls, remote monitoring, cloud integration paths
  
- ❓ **Passive Cooling Products (Dry Coolers, Fan Walls):** Possibly out-of-scope
  - Reasoning: May lack digital elements if purely mechanical; need specification confirmation

**Compliance Obligations (Effective Dec 11, 2027):**

**Article 6 – Security-by-Design:** Manufacturers must:
- Implement security-by-design and security-by-default principles in product development
- Conduct security risk assessments
- Develop and maintain incident response procedures
- Publish SBOMs (Software Bills of Materials) accessible to customers and authorities

**Article 7 – Classification (Class I vs Class II):**
- **Class I (Lower Risk):** Default category; requires basic compliance measures
- **Class II (Higher Risk):** Products identified as "high-risk" (e.g., physical safety implications, critical infrastructure); requires enhanced security measures, third-party conformity assessment
