# Case Study: Critical Rail Infrastructure — IEC 62443 Cybersecurity Case Delivery

## The Challenge
A major critical rail infrastructure programme in New Zealand — five years in development — had deferred its cybersecurity workstream while civil and systems engineering progressed. By the time OXOT's principal was engaged to lead the cybersecurity programme, the project was behind schedule and facing a familiar problem: years of compliance work still to be done, compressed into months.

The deliverable was a complete IEC 62443 / TS 50701 Cybersecurity Case Dossier: 3 Volumes, 8 Sections, 101 formally controlled documents. These included Threat Vulnerability Assessments, Traceability Matrices, System-Level Architecture narratives, a Cybersecurity Management Plan, Security Responsibility Assignment Controls, Zone/Conduit decomposition models, and specialised Basis documents mapping the intersection of safety, hazard traceability, FMECA/MITRE analysis, and Security Level Target assignments. The programme required delivery in approximately five months — a timeline that would be unrealistic under a conventional consulting approach.

## The OXOT Approach
The engagement was people-led and tool-enabled. The senior engineer focused on what no platform can replace: building consensus with the client's engineering team, the safety assessor, the independent verifier, and multiple system integration contractors. Understanding the operational reality of the system and making sound engineering judgements required trust, domain knowledge, and direct engagement with the people who run the infrastructure.

OXOT's proprietary programme management and assessment platform handled the heavy lifting — automated compliance mapping, cross-document consistency verification, version control across 101 interdependent documents, and systematic gap identification. The platform ran multiple verification processes in parallel: compliance checking against every IEC 62443-3-3 and TS 50701 clause, engineering rationalisation of SL-T assignments across FMECA and MITRE mappings, and document integrity governance across the full manifest. When legacy SL-T designations were found inconsistent with mandated requirements, corrections were propagated across all 92 impacted artifacts simultaneously with full audit trail.

This division of labour — senior engineer on relationships and engineering judgement, tooling platform on systematic verification and document governance — is what made the compressed timeline possible.

## Outcomes
- **Five-month delivery** of a regulator-ready Cybersecurity Case Dossier that met independent assessment requirements without rework, remediation requests, or supplementary evidence demands
- **Cross-referential integrity** across 101 interdependent documents — anomalies that manual review would have missed were detected and corrected systematically
- **Full traceability** — every modification, gap identification, and remediation action logged and attributable
- **Cost and staffing efficiency** — a conventional firm would have staffed this with 8-12 analysts over 12-18 months; OXOT delivered with one senior engineer and a tooling platform, faster and with greater accuracy
