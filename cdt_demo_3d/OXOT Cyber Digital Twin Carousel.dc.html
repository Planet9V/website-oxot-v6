THE METHOD
10 / 15

We build the model.
Then we break it — ten thousand times.

An engineering twin tells you what exists. A cyber digital twin tells you what it costs you when it fails, how likely that is this year, and which three things to fix first.

01BUILD the as-is
Geometry, topology and control layer, reconciled against drawings, config and a site walk. The twin is the agreed statement of what is actually installed.
02ENRICH with the BOM
Every object inherits its hardware, software, firmware, cryptographic and service bill of materials — then that BOM is cross-referenced against live vulnerability and threat intelligence.
03SIMULATE the consequence
Threat-model each pathway, breach the minimum operating requirements, and run the outcome distribution — not a single worst case, and not a heat-map colour.
04PRIORITISE the spend
Rank every finding by loss avoided per euro spent, and defend the ranking in front of a board, an insurer or a regulator.

STEP 04 · THE OUTPUT
14 / 15

NOW · NEXT · NEVER

Not a severity score. A defensible spending order, derived from reachability in the twin, exploitability in the wild, and loss avoided per euro.

NOW
Reachable, exploited, and it breaks a minimum requirement.
Known-exploited vulnerability on an asset with a live pathway to a safety or cooling function. Highest loss avoided per euro. Fix inside the change window.
NEXT
Real, but the consequence is bounded or the path is long.
Meaningful ALE reduction that does not justify an emergency outage. Scheduled into the next maintenance window, with a compensating control until then.
NEVER
Unreachable, or the fix costs more than the loss.
Accepted with reasoning recorded against the object in the twin. The most valuable column — it is what lets your team defensibly stop working on the other 400 findings.
Re-run it when anything changes.
New CVE, new firmware, new tenant load, a decommissioned UPS — the pathway analysis and the ALE move with the facility, because they were never separate from it.
