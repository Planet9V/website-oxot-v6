Why this example

A hyperscale datacentre, because it is hard

It has everything that makes OT risk difficult in one place: a power train from grid intake to rack tray, liquid cooling with a shared central facility, life-safety systems on hardwired interlocks, and an IT estate sitting right next to all of it.

The campus is split into two independent cells, which is how hyperscalers actually build. That gives the demo something worth asking: does a compromise in one cell stay in that cell?

Your facility will look nothing like this. The process is the same.

Power

Grid intake through MV switchgear, transformers, UPS, generators and per-cell busway to the rack.

Cooling

Central chillers and towers feeding row CDUs, with direct-to-chip loops into each rack.

IT & network

Carrier demarcs, firewalls, dual spine, per-row distribution, jumpbox and out-of-band console.

OT boundary

Per-cell OT switch and firewall, data diode, protocol gateway, passive monitoring from the NOC.

Life safety

Fire panels, aspirating detection, suppression and the hardwired interlocks that bypass the network.

Physical security

Perimeter detection, mantrap, cameras and door contacts back to the security officer's desk.

What this demo is not

This page is a walkthrough of the onboarding process, running on an example facility we built ourselves. It is deliberately limited — enough interaction to show what the method feels like, and no more.

The Cyber Digital Twin itself carries your live asset data, your configurations, your bills of material and your threat intelligence, and produces the full artifact set. That is a different thing, and we show it separately.
