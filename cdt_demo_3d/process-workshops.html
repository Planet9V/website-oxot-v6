TECHNICAL GLOSSARY & STANDARDS
CORE CONCEPTS

Methodology Framework Terms

IEC 62443 & EU CRA READY
Term / Acronym Engineering Definition & Application in Digital Twin
ATQ Attacker Threat Quotient: Our sector-specific threat framing methodology that weights comparative adversary capabilities by facility type, equipment classes, and geographic jurisdiction, replacing generic worst-case assumptions with defensible likelihood metrics.
TACAM Target Attack Consequence & Asset Mapping: Threat actor and attack pathway taxonomy carrying typical vulnerabilities across OT equipment classes (PLCs, BMS, UPS, CDUs) and mapping them directly to physical network dependencies.
ZCR Zone & Conduit Requirements: The flattened network and logical topology view of the digital twin, establishing zone boundaries, conduit inspection requirements, and target security levels per IEC 62443-3-2.
SL-T / SL-C / SL-A Security Level Framework: SL-T (Target) is the required security level based on consequence; SL-C (Capability) is what security countermeasures can reasonably achieve; SL-A (Achieved) is the validated real-world state of the facility.
ALARP / SFAIRP As Low As Reasonably Practicable / So Far As Is Reasonably Practicable: The legal and engineering test applied to residual risk gaps to prove that the cost of additional mitigation is grossly disproportionate to the risk reduction gained.
Critical Conduit A network connection crossing an IEC 62443 zone boundary (especially between different Purdue levels or security domains) requiring explicit logging, filtering, or cryptographic protection under IEC 62443-3-2 §5.6.
Cell Architecture A self-contained 5–10 MW electrical and thermal block within a hyperscale facility featuring independent power distribution, CDU loops, and OT VLANs with data diode isolation.
PREPARATION & GOVERNANCE

Frequently Asked Questions

Only standard engineering documentation is required: piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs), single-line power schematics (SLDs), controller equipment lists, network architecture drawings, and Interface Control Documents (ICDs). No specialized formatting or pre-processing is needed; the Seldon Engine parses standard engineering formats directly.
Engineering documentation and real-world installations inevitably drift over time. Floor Walk 1 (during Workshop 1) reconciles the baseline twin against physical field reality (uncovering unmanaged maintenance ports and ghost assets). Floor Walk 2 (during Phase 3) validates the high-fidelity twin against active switch configurations and physical firewall barriers before delivering audit-grade reports.
Under the As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP) principle, the digital twin quantifies the diminishing marginal return of security spending. Once the disproportion factor is reached, the decision to accept residual risk is mathematically justified and documented in the executive risk memo (A8), providing legal defensibility under EU CRA and IEC 62443 audits.
